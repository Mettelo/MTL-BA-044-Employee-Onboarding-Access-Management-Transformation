# Stakeholder and elicitation source pack
**Mettelo BA-044 · Stage 2 · Interviews 8–10 July; workshop 13 July**
All people and dialogue are fictional. Organisational relationships are source context, not a completed stakeholder assessment or future RACI.

Sarah Bennett sponsors People change. Grace Mills leads HR operations, Imran Ali the IT service team, Chloe Turner Security and Rob Lewis Facilities. James Brown manages consulting delivery; Maya Evans is a synthetic new starter linked to J0211. Nisha Patel owns a company application; Omar Reed coordinates client access. Each has different authority and incentives.

## 1. Grace Mills — HR Manager
Grace owns approved employment facts, contracts and effective dates. She is concerned that staff describe HR as late when the authoritative date has changed correctly but no downstream acknowledgement exists.

**Interviewer:** What happened in J0211?
**Grace:** “The original date was 10 June and was brought forward to 8 June. The request was in on 1 June. HR corrected its record, but the closed IT ticket did not prove anyone had accepted the change. The laptop arrived on 10 June and core login was recorded on 11 June.”

She cannot provide exact export/acknowledgement timestamps for every change. The snapshot shows original and effective dates, not a full event history. She wants a way to know who has acted on a change, but has not approved a future system or data model.

**Interviewer:** Can every team see the HR record?
**Grace:** “No. Facilities needs desk/pass and relevant delivery coordination, not salary or medical information. Home addresses should stay in an approved restricted delivery route. A portal with everything visible to everyone would be a different problem.”

Grace wants earlier notification and fewer duplicate requests, then recognises that managers may not know final client assignment at contract approval. A future process must distinguish stable facts from later decisions and provide an explicit exception, not force invented data.

**Interviewer:** What would you validate?
**Grace:** “Source-date ownership, the HR change event and a representative set of corrections. I cannot approve privileged access or client applications. Sarah's sponsorship is not a replacement for those approvals.”

Unresolved: no complete retention schedule, date-change event log or full role population is supplied. Grace offers one focused weekly session and expects the BA to distinguish missing evidence, unacknowledged changes and genuine late submission. She has not signed a future workflow or a 95% readiness definition.

## 2. Imran Ali — IT Service Manager
Imran owns service queues, company accounts and laptop provisioning. His team handles incidents as well as joiners and is assessed partly on ticket timeliness.

**Interviewer:** Why is a closed ticket not ready?
**Imran:** “Closure currently accepts a dispatch reference and a created account. We do not require the worker to log in or every application owner to confirm. J0212 was closed 5 June, laptop delivered 9 June and core login recorded 9 June after an 8 June start. The closure metric is doing what it was defined to do; it is not the whole outcome.”

He wants the project to be clear about who confirms a business role versus who applies a technical entitlement. Copying a colleague's access may be quick but can carry unnecessary or privileged permissions. A new screen cannot choose appropriate client access without owners.

**Interviewer:** Would mandatory fields solve it?
**Imran:** “They could stop incomplete requests, but they could also cause placeholders. Some applications are not known until a client assignment is confirmed. We need a controlled pending/exception route and an owner, not just a hard block.”

He asks for an earlier lead time, then acknowledges that effective dates can change. A rule that ignores an approved correction would create a different failure. He cannot promise every courier delivery from a ticket-closure date.

**Interviewer:** What happens when a leaver account is disabled?
**Imran:** “The directory event is one fact. Independent SaaS, sessions and passes may need separate action. The service board can show done without evidence of every permission. We need the owners involved.”

Unresolved: complete independent-app inventory, exact active-session behaviour and all courier events are absent. Imran can demonstrate current tools but will not commit capacity without prioritisation. Existing licence does not mean zero-cost configuration or proof of whole-service readiness.

## 3. Chloe Turner — Information Security Manager
Chloe owns access-control policy and emergency/privileged exceptions. She supports mobilisation but rejects pressure to give every starter broad access before evidence is available.

**Interviewer:** What is the main control concern?
**Chloe:** “Appropriate business need, authorised owner, correct role and review/expiry. A manager's need statement is not the same as application-owner approval. A technical account-created event is not proof the worker had only the permissions required.”

The access extract has source approval evidence and blank expiry fields. Chloe says blanks show unavailable retained expiry evidence, not a conclusion that every entitlement is active forever. She requests an investigation and a controlled trial rather than a mass assertion of breach.

**Interviewer:** Explain the leaver example.
**Chloe:** “L0081's directory was disabled 1 June at 17:00; independent SaaS revocation is recorded 4 June at 09:00. That is a reason to examine ownership and timing. L0082 left at noon on 2 June but the directory event is 17:00. The default-time instruction matters, but do not generalise two cases to every leaver.”

She wants source time and timezone explicit and unverified access visible. A blank revocation is not proof of continuing access, but it is not assurance that access ended either.

**Interviewer:** Can emergency access be flexible?
**Chloe:** “With the appropriate recorded authority, time limit and review. A manager cannot approve their own privileged exception because a client start is urgent. Do not turn a service target into policy waiver.”

Unresolved: no complete entitlement/session population or audit certification exists. Chloe will review focused rules and tests, not sign a supplier demo as secure. She expects rollback, correction and review evidence, and warns that a single ready tick may hide distinct control decisions.

## 4. James Brown — consulting line manager
James is accountable for client mobilisation and delivery capacity. He says the current form does not describe the actual work his people need to do.

**Interviewer:** Why are access requests late?
**James:** “The worker may be hired before the client assignment is final. I can approve the business reason but not make the client owner approve faster. A departmental package gets the laptop and email right but misses the client portal.”

He admits using an old spreadsheet because it contains application names. Sometimes he replies approve to an email without knowing whether it was attached to the ticket. He wants a clear acknowledgement and a way to see the remaining owner rather than resubmit everything.

**Interviewer:** Would same-as-colleague help?
**James:** “It sounds convenient, but Chloe is right that colleagues accumulate access. I need a sensible role approach with an exception for actual client needs, not a copied entitlement list. I cannot design or authorise the technical permissions myself.”

He initially asks for all requests to be complete before a contract is signed, then recognises that assignment changes make that unrealistic. He wants early stable information and controlled later updates.

**Interviewer:** What should ready mean?
**James:** “Able to perform the agreed work, not a box showing dispatched. But we need a defined starter scope and evidence. A worker may have core access and still lack a required client application.”

Unresolved: no full billable-days or lost-margin dataset is provided. James can review a contained cohort but cannot promise client-system access or salary savings. He wants the BA to show how additional approval/review work would be supported rather than hide it in a deadline.

## 5. Maya Evans — synthetic starter, J0211
Maya represents one remote starter, not every worker. The account uses no real address, salary or identity information.

**Interviewer:** What did ready look like from your side?
**Maya:** “The start moved to 8 June. I saw a message saying the setup was complete, but the laptop arrived on the old date, 10 June. Login was recorded on 11 June. I did not know who could correct it or whether I should ask HR, IT or my manager.”

She watched induction through a colleague's screen but did not receive their password. The account is not permission to share credentials. She wants a named contact, an understandable next action and a way to report that a completed task did not achieve its outcome.

**Interviewer:** Would a portal have helped?
**Maya:** “If it agreed with the date I had been given and showed what I could do. Another checklist saying complete would not. I also would not want delivery or personal information visible to people who do not need it.”

She asks for clear activation instructions and an accessible support route. Her experience cannot establish how many other delays occurred or whether every step could have been ready earlier.

**Interviewer:** What matters after first login?
**Maya:** “Whether I can do the agreed work. Email working is not the same as expenses or the client system working. It should be clear which access is pending and who will help.”

Unresolved: the source provides event dates, not complete message receipts, device traces or a representative satisfaction study. Maya has not validated a future design or approved how employment information is processed. The BA should investigate contrasting office/client-site experiences and separate onboarding communication from access authority.

## 6. Rob Lewis — Facilities Manager
Rob owns passes, desks and office arrangements. His team has limited access to HR/IT information and maintains a local register.

**Interviewer:** What information is missing?
**Rob:** “Effective date, site and whether an apparently remote starter is attending induction. A remote worker may still need a pass. I sometimes see unnecessary personal detail while missing the change that affects a desk.”

He wants controlled access to relevant facts and an acknowledgement when dates or locations change. He does not want unrestricted HR records or responsibility for every courier problem. Courier dispatch, delivery and physical handover need different evidence.

**Interviewer:** What about leavers?
**Rob:** “Pass removal is separate from directory disable. The instruction often just says leaving date; a midday or immediate departure needs the effective time. L0082 is a useful source example. Do not assume 17:00 is always right.”

Rob asks for integration, then says a reliable controlled notification might be sufficient if ownership and acknowledgement are clear. He wants the BA to establish the actual need rather than assume an expensive connector.

Unresolved: no full desk-utilisation, courier-loss or pass-access population is supplied. Rob can review a small office/remote contrast but not approve application entitlements. He wants workable fallback and transition for open tasks, not an apparently complete new process that ignores the local register.

## 7. Nisha Patel — application owner
Nisha owns approval for a company application. She decides which business roles are appropriate within her authority, while IT carries out provisioning.

**Interviewer:** Why does manager approval not finish the request?
**Nisha:** “A manager confirms need. I need to know the role, purpose and whether elevated access is justified. The source sometimes has manager approval but no application-owner event. That can mean waiting, evidence elsewhere or a bypass; investigate before choosing the explanation.”

She wants enough context to decide without collecting unnecessary worker information. A form saying other or same as colleague may not establish the need. She also wants changes after approval linked to the relevant decision rather than overwrite.

**Interviewer:** What evidence should remain?
**Nisha:** “Who requested, who approved what and the conditions/review. I do not mean retain everything forever. The retention owner needs to be involved. A tick is not useful if nobody can identify the permission it applies to.”

She supports pre-approved patterns where validated, but the pack supplies no approved role/access matrix. She refuses to treat a supplier's catalogue as her permission model.

Unresolved: complete role inventory, access reviews and all historical approvals are not supplied. Nisha offers a focused review of proposals and exceptions. She expects a clear way to revoke/correct and evidence that the worker has appropriate usable access, not simply an account-created event.

## 8. Omar Reed — client access coordinator
Omar coordinates with external client-system owners. Harrowgate can request access but cannot always grant it directly.

**Interviewer:** Why is client access different?
**Omar:** “The client may require named approval or training. Our manager's request and IT's ticket do not establish the client approved it. A portal that marks complete when it sends an email can hide that dependency.”

He wants ownership of outstanding requests and a route for corrected dates/assignments. A worker changing projects can need some access removed and other access added; a joiner-only design may leave unnecessary permissions behind.

**Interviewer:** Can the process promise day-one access?
**Omar:** “Only within clear scope and assumptions. We should know what is required, when information is available and what the external owner commits to. Do not promise a client response time without evidence.”

He says urgent requests are common but cannot quantify a representative rate. He rejects shared credentials as a shortcut and asks that emergency requests remain subject to appropriate authority and expiry.

Unresolved: external owner commitments, complete application inventory and offboarding confirmation for every client are absent. Omar can review scenarios but cannot approve another organisation's policy. The BA should make dependencies visible, propose a credible staged scope and explain what readiness can actually mean.

## Workshop extract — 13 July
Sarah asks for one ready view. Grace asks which effective date is authoritative; Imran distinguishes closure from use; Chloe asks about privilege and independent revocation; James asks who owns client delays; Maya wants a useful next action; Rob wants relevant information only; Nisha distinguishes need from entitlement; Omar identifies external authority.

No future RACI, access matrix, portal, readiness definition or target was approved. The chair requests a BA playback separating facts, conflicts, dependencies and validation needs. Further questions must identify the decision they affect; unavailable evidence remains unresolved.
